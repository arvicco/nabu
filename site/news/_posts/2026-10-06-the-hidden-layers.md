description: >-
  A wrap-up phase that parsed what was already on the shelves: the
  Pali canon's variant apparatus and concordances, full morphology
  and real dates for the Middle German reference corpora, the Hebrew
  Bible's complete word-level detail with a Dead Sea Scrolls
  crosswalk, and the lexicostatistical wordlists that shipped inside
  the Tower of Babel packages twenty years ago. Two new grants open
  the next desks.

Most phases grow the library outward. This one grew it inward: almost
everything below was already sitting in the fetched trees, unread.

**The Pali canon gets its apparatus.** SuttaCentral's bilara-data has
always shipped three sidecar trees beside the root texts, keyed to
the very same segment ids — and the library had never read them. Now
every root passage of the Tipiṭaka carries what its segment attests:
19,873 variant readings ("anabhiratisaññā → anabhiratasaññā (bj)"),
142,865 concordance strings mapping segments to PTS volume-and-page,
VRI, the Burmese/Thai/Sinhala print editions and Taishō lines — the
crosswalk that lets PTS-citing scholarship resolve into held passages
— and some eleven thousand translator notes from the English
translations, riding the root segments they explain. No new fetch was
needed; the data was in the clone all along.

**Middle High and Middle Low German grow up.** The two German
reference corpora (ReM and ReN, 406 and 235 texts, together some
650,000 passages) parse a TEI export whose upstream deposits carry a
richer sibling: the project's own CorA-XML layer. The fetch now
brings both (CC BY 4.0 deposits, checksum-pinned), and with them:
full part-of-speech and inflection morphology on all 2.58 million
Middle High German tokens; real citation-form lemmas — the TEI
attribute turns out to carry normalized word forms, while the true
dictionary forms ("wër(e)lt", "dër", each with its Middle High German
dictionary id) live only in the CorA layer, and the lemma lane now
serves them; and dating for both corpora, which had stood as
"undatable" — 396 of 406 ReM texts and all 235 ReN texts now carry
their century-grid or year dates, with scriptoria and dialect areas
where the headers name them.

**The Hebrew Bible, in full detail.** The ETCBC's BHSA database ships
about ninety word-level features; the library rode nine. It now rides
every word-grain scalar the dataset publishes — 44 newly carried,
from the pointed morpheme segmentation to lexical statistics — each
under its upstream name, with every deliberate omission documented in
the adapter rather than silently dropped. And the Dead Sea Scrolls
corpus gains its ETCBC-harmonized layer, including the lexeme
crosswalk: 422,325 of 422,424 scroll words — 100.0% — now resolve to
their Hebrew Bible lexeme, so a form in the Temple Scroll joins hands
with its biblical counterpart by plain equality.

**The wordlists in the attic.** The Tower of Babel's Indo-European
package has carried a LEXSTAT directory since 2005: thirteen
lexicostatistical tables — ten Swadesh-grain wordlists (Baltic,
Celtic, Dardic, Germanic, Indo-Aryan, Iranian, Romance, Slavic, a
mixed list, and the Proto-Indo-European union table) whose every form
carries a cognation number chaining into the etymological databases,
plus three Indo-Iranian etymology tables. Fetched long ago, parsed
never — until now: 24,522 entries across thirteen new shelves,
credited to the package's compilers, S. L. Nikolayev and
S. A. Starostin. The wider licence ground firmed up the same week:
George Starostin confirmed by email (2026-10-01) that all remaining
downloadable StarlingDB databases fall under the original grant's
terms, with the works-in-progress and the Nostratic base explicitly
reserved by their authors.

**The Diet Library crawl continues.** The National Diet Library
shelf advanced to 8,789 books of its 97,731-item census, at the
committed gentle pace, refusals ledgered and never retried.

**Two desks open next.** Jost Gippert extended the TITUS personal-use
arrangement a second time (by email, 2026-10-06): the Tocharian A and
B corpora from the Berlin Turfan collection — the only complete
digital text set of the manuscripts — and the Middle Iranian
families: the Pahlavi books, the Manichaean Middle Persian and
Parthian texts, Sogdian, Khotanese and Bactrian. The terms are the
ones three grants have now shared: one retrieval per corpus, local
use only, nothing redistributed, TITUS and the editors credited
wherever a passage is displayed. The harvest is queued; Iron Age
Anatolia's eastern neighbors are on their way.

**The numbers, as of this gate:** 2,314,941 documents · 107,833,142
passages · 174 live sources.
