title: "A place ruled, the dictionaries grouped, and a canon with names"
date: 2026-09-27 18:30:00 +0000
description: >-
  The Chinese place-name survey produced its first ruled identity and
  a review board for nine thousand more; the reference shelf's 2.6
  million entries now group by language lineage; and the Tibetan
  canon's four and a half thousand texts finally carry their titles —
  with fifty-six thousand glossary terms beside them.

Three threads landed this week: the places program crossed from
mining to ruling, the dictionary shelf learned its own family tree,
and the Tibetan canon got its names.

**The first ruled place.** The library's Chinese texts have been
mined against the CHGIS historical gazetteer for a while — 2.97
million candidate sightings of nearly ten thousand distinct
place-names across the Kanseki Repository's five thousand texts. What was missing was
judgment. The new review board lays each name out with its gazetteer
candidates, dynastic-era discriminators and passage snippets, and
verdicts now flow into a small curated registry: forty
frequent-but-false names — bureaucratic titles, personal names,
common nouns that happen to spell like villages — are stopped at the
source, and 杜陵 — Du Ling, the Han-era county southeast of
Chang'an where the classicist Tian He settled — became the first
name ruled to a single gazetteer identity. Its 5,443 passage
attestations are now first-class place links, readable from either
end. One name down; the board holds the queue for the rest.

**Dictionaries now group by lineage.** The reference shelf has grown
to 465 dictionaries and 2.6 million entries, shipped under dozens of
language codes — Assyriologists' period codes, Wiktionary's
etymology-only codes, plain ISO. A new precompiled census resolves
every shelf through the lect registry, so a lookup in *Latin* now
knows that the medieval-Latin shelf belongs to it, and the language
card for Akkadian can show its whole dictionary family at once. The
registry itself grew to meet the census: the full ORACC Akkadian
dialect grid (Old Assyrian through Late Babylonian, Standard
Babylonian as the literary register, the peripheral Akkadian of the
Amarna age), Emesal — the Sumerian cult-lament register — and
anchors for Ugaritic, Etruscan, Lepontic, Sabine, Canaano-Akkadian
and Old Japanese, each with its classification and attestation
recorded. Codes the registry cannot honestly place stay counted and
labeled as unresolved rather than guessed.

**The Tibetan canon has titles now.** The Derge Kangyur and Tengyur
arrived as exact woodblock reproductions — 4,560 texts identified
only by their Tohoku catalog numbers. But the 84000 translation
project's files name every one of those texts in English and Tibetan,
including the thousands not yet translated, whose placeholder records
exist precisely to hold the titles. A crosswalk now reads those
headers and titles 4,451 of the 4,560 documents: Toh 767 is no longer
a bare number but the *Tantra of the Supreme Dancer of the Yakṣas
(gnod sbyin gar mkhan mchog gyi rgyud)*. And the same files' back
matter — glossaries the parser had always stepped around — yielded
56,794 term records linking Tibetan, Sanskrit and English
(*garuḍa* / གསེར་འདབ / gser 'dab), now a searchable shelf of its own
beside the Mahāvyutpatti. The entries carry 84000's CC BY-NC-ND
grant, credited as always.
