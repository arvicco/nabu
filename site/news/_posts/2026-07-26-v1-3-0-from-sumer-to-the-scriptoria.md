Version 1.3.0 is tagged — the largest span between any two tags of
this library: from 801 thousand documents / 28.2 million passages at
v1.2.0 (22 July) to **947,273 documents / 66,755,518 passages across
96 sources** four days later.

**The Arabic phase** more than doubled the corpus in a day: OpenITI,
the premodern Islamicate library whole — hadith, history, falsafa,
the dīwāns — with its Persian shelf, making Classical Arabic the
largest language on the shelves (33.3 million passages).

**The instruments phase** gave verse a meter layer (167,732 scanned
lines; `search --meter H` filters to hexameters and *Iliad* 1.1 knows
what it is), moved the Pleiades gazetteer into a derived index
(place lookups from seconds to milliseconds), and finished the MCP
surface's user pass — eleven read-only tools.

**The Romance phase** opened the twenty-first desk: the MGH critical
editions, digilibLT's late-antique prose, the Base de français
médiéval from the *Serments de Strasbourg* onward, the UD Romance
treebanks — four new gold languages in a day, CroALa dual-tagged as
the continuum's Latin side.

**The Rabbinic and Geʿez phase** closed the span: the Mishnah in
three complete Hebrew editions, both Talmuds with daf-grain
citations, the Tosefta; the first Ethiopic shelf — 1 Enoch and
Jubilees, complete only in Geʿez, with Dillmann's lexicon and the
TraCES gold corpus — under the twenty-second desk; the inscriptions
of Italy (EDR, 115,590 records); Urartian, gold-lemmatized; Middle
Low German; and the comparativist loanword layer (WOLD, CLICS³, the
StarLing Kartvelian base).

The census at the tag, all live-read on 26 July 2026: **19,223,970
gold lemma rows in thirty-seven languages** (28.1 million more at a
labelled silver tier), **1,391,759 dictionary entries on one hundred
shelves**, 549,771 dated-and-placed documents on the timeline, and a
written dossier behind every one of the 305 held language codes.

As ever: local, license-honest per document, rebuildable from
canonical bytes, and readable — never writable — by the AI tools at
the researcher's desk.
