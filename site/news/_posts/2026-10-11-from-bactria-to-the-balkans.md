Two phases, two new wings. The library's Middle Iranian shelf is now
complete as TITUS hosts it: five grant-gated corpora — the Pahlavi
books, the Manichaean corpora, Sogdian, Khotanese Saka and Bactrian —
joined Tocharian A in one wave, each retrieved once under its
personal grant, census-fixed against the real pages, and verified to
a clean parse. The wave's quiet heroes are the belts: not one of the
191 first-sync surprises (unknown editorial lanes, severed UTF-8,
header-only pages, the editors' own "not yet read" entries) aborted a
sync — every one quarantined honestly, got its census, and recovered.
Bactrian's dated documents now carry their own era-year formulas,
mined raw until the contested epoch (de Blois's 223 vs Schindel's
227/8) gets a ruled envelope; 417 of the Tocharian A leaves know
their findspot.

The second wing opens the Slavic shelves. The KorBa manually
annotated Middle Polish subcorpus and the 1488 Prague Bible ground
truth anchor the West; nine wikisource cones — Serbian epic song to
Old East Slavic chronicle — were censused category by category, with
the modern reworkings, translations and post-1930 editions excluded
by written rules (the Russian cone's honest yield: 12 texts, after
31 pages keyed to in-copyright editions were turned away). Riding
along: Brückner's 1927 etymological dictionary of Polish as a real,
queryable shelf — 5,711 headwords from the proofread scans.

The al-Ṯurayyā gazetteer of the early Islamic world joined the place
apparatus (2,331 places, Arabic and transliterated names), the
incremental rebuild's identity sweep got a memoized fast path, and
the registry grew its West and South Slavic families — Old and
Middle Polish, Old and Middle Czech, the Church Slavonic recensions
documented as varieties.

By the numbers: eleven new text sources this round — ten live on
their first pass (the Serbian cone alone carries 4,607 epic songs,
charters and manuscript pages in 581 thousand verse-line passages;
the Macedonian cone 836 texts of the Miladinov generation), one
waiting out its publisher's server trouble. The two batch-size
surprises the Macedonian and Serbian walks uncovered were fixed the
same day and both corpora recovered in full.

As always: canonical means canonical — every text verbatim from its
source, every exclusion a written rule, every license the grantor's
own words.
