title: "The annals, the fathers, and the honest instruments"
date: 2026-08-21 06:00:00 +0000
description: >-
  Three phases in one arc: the Korean desk opens with half a
  millennium of Joseon state records, the instruments get honest —
  search stops inventing Greek fragments, erased papyrus text
  returns in double brackets — and the Patrologia Latina brings the
  church fathers home beside Catalan print, Portuguese drama, and
  the small Romance voices.
---

Three waves since the vernaculars arrived, and the library now holds
**988,556 documents — 73.6 million passages — in more than 130
languages.**

**The Korean desk.** The Veritable Records of the Joseon Dynasty —
five hundred years of daily court annals in literary Chinese — enter
beside the Daily Records of the Royal Secretariat (1.9 million
passages, the desk's deep water), the Goryeosa, the Border Defense
Command registers, and the classical works of the Institute for the
Translation of Korean Classics. The vernacular leg opens with the
Songs of the Dragons Flying to Heaven, the first work ever printed
in hangul (1447), its archaic letters and tone marks preserved
byte-for-byte. And the Đại Việt chronicles ride the sinitic shelf,
as they should: the classical Vietnamese court wrote in the same
literary Chinese as its neighbors.

**The honest instruments.** A phase spent on the tools themselves.
Search no longer lets one language's edition markers mangle another
language's letters — the fix that ended Greek queries surfacing
numeral fragments above real words. Erased-but-legible papyrus text
now renders in the papyrologist's double brackets, ⟦so⟧, corpus-wide:
nearly five thousand cancelled lines returned to citability, because
what a scribe crossed out is still evidence. And every source now
declares an honest answer to "is your upstream still there?" — no
more probes pretending a zip archive is a git repository.

**The Latin heart, and the small voices.** Migne's Patrologia Latina
— 5,248 works of the church fathers, 85 million words, Augustine to
the medieval sermon collections — is the largest single shelf the
library has ever gained. Around it, a Romance wave: a thousand
public-domain Catalan works with their dialect variants recorded,
Portuguese literature from Gil Vicente's 1517 boat plays onward,
Early New High German manuscripts at manuscript-line grain, and the
small voices that rarely get shelves at all — Ladino newspaper
prose, Sardinian sentences, Occitan in six dialects side by side,
and Aranese, the Val d'Aran's own Gascon.

The doors keep opening, too: the Menota archive of Nordic
manuscripts and Michigan's Corpus of Middle English both granted
access this week — next waves, already queued.
