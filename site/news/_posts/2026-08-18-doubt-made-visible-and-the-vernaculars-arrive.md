Two waves since the layers learned to join, one epistemic and one
territorial.

**The library now says how sure it is — and only when it isn't.** The
uncertainty doctrine has three house tiers — *certain · probable ·
uncertain* — with two rules that keep it honest: absence is not a tier
(an undated document stays honestly undated, it is not "uncertain"),
and precision is not certainty (a wide date band is wide, not
doubtful). The render axiom does the work: **certain is silent,
deviation is labeled, and the upstream's own word always rides
verbatim beside the house word.** A place card says `probable
("presumably")` — the gloss and the source's hedge together, a
render-time label that never rewrites what upstream said. The same
object rides the MCP payloads, merged only when something is actually
below certain, so machine consumers pay nothing for confidence.

**The medieval vernaculars arrive.** The largest wave of new languages
since the library opened:

- **Old Spanish**, via the Hispanic Seminary's Old Spanish Textual
  Archive — semi-paleographic transcriptions of medieval manuscripts
  kept verbatim (the curly-brace transcription convention parsed, not
  flattened), with a lemmatized sibling lane. The archive's own works
  table resolves each codex's *lengua*: alongside the Castilian bulk,
  the Leonese texts carry Old Asturian and the Navarro-Aragonese ones
  Old Aragonese — two stages minted for the occasion in the
  [nabu-lects](https://arvicco.github.io/nabu-lects) registry.
- **Old Italian** — the full Commedia as a dependency treebank, all
  122,038 words, while the big Old Italian corpora remain walled.
- **Old Swedish** — the laws, religious prose and rhymed chronicles of
  Fornsvenska textbanken, under the new `sv:old` stage.
- **The Spanish sonnet corpus** — 4,530 sonnets across five centuries.
- **Achaemenid Babylonian** — 2,830 legal and administrative texts
  from the Persian-empire archives (the Murašû firm of Nippur, the
  Eanna temple of Uruk), lemmatized by the Helsinki team's pipeline
  and carried at the honest *silver* tier: automatic annotation is
  labeled as such, never passed off as gold.
- **An Etruscan concordance** — Annie Burman's crosswalk of fourteen
  citation systems (Trismegistos, CIE, Rix, TLE, CIL…) joins as a
  links instrument, so an inscription's whole identity trail answers
  from one query.

**And the library re-proved it can die — this time all the way.** The
restore drill (back up the three permanent folders, restore to a bare
machine, rebuild everything derived, compare) had never run fully
green on the live corpus; its first serious runs found real
nondeterminism — two canonical inputs claiming one identity, a
catalog's citation markers minting phantom documents. Those became a
five-layer determinism contract, the drill grew a space guard and
per-stage narration with honest progress, and the proof now closes:
977,241 documents and 68.6 million passages, derived twice,
byte-identical. The catalog is a pure function of the sources, the
decisions, and the code — demonstrated, not asserted.

Alongside: graded reading reached cuneiform (`search --signset "ŠEŠ,
AK"` finds real tablet passages a learner's sign inventory can
actually read, the sign-list analog of the Han `--charset` lane), and
the first public hieroglyph frequency table — Gardiner-code counts
from a gold-annotated corpus, per genre — joined the open datasets.
