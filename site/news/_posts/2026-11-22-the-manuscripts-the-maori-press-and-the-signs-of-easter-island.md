The last news item put the critical apparatus around the Greek New
Testament — five editions, word by word, each reading tagged with the
editions that attest it. Behind every one of those editions stand the
manuscripts, and now the manuscripts themselves are here.

**CNTR — the Center for New Testament Restoration's transcriptions**
(Alan Bunning): **189 witnesses / 42,228 verse passages** — 130
continuous-text papyri and majuscules through the fourth century (P52,
P66, P75, P46; Sinaiticus, Vaticanus, Alexandrinus, Ephraemi,
Bezae…), 47 extant manuscripts quoting the NT (amulets, letters,
lectionary and commentary fragments), and 12 church fathers'
reconstructed exemplars, all in word-level Manuscript Encoding
Specification markup. The text given is the first hand's reading with
restorations read through and lacunae marked; later hands,
corrections and editorial detail ride as annotations. All 189
witnesses join the NT alignment hub, so a verse now answers with its
editions *and* its manuscripts side by side: P52's fragment of John
18 next to the five editions that print the verse. CC BY-SA 4.0, with
the CNTR credit on every passage.

**The Pacific shelf opens** — a new desk in the library, and both of
its new members arrive by permission rather than public license:

- **Niupepa — Māori Newspapers** (the Niupepa collection of the New
  Zealand Digital Library, University of Waikato): 34 newspaper
  series, 1,818 issues spanning **1842–1932** — the largest corpus of
  historical written Māori. The harvest is deliberately slow: a
  polite, paced crawl spread over weeks, with the **first 134 issues
  / 1,313 page passages** already searchable, from the 1842 *Te
  Karere o Nui Tireni* onward. Held as a personal research corpus,
  local only and never redistributed, with the collection and the
  NZDL credited on every passage.
- **The CEIPP rongorongo corpus** (kohaumotu.org): Thomas Barthel's
  numeric transliteration of the **25 surviving inscribed objects**
  of Easter Island — twenty tablets, the Santiago Staff, two reimiro,
  the tangata manu and the snuff box — as extended and edited by the
  C.E.I.P.P.'s Rongorongo Commission: **314 lines / 15,273 coded
  glyphs**. Rongorongo is undeciphered; the library takes the
  transliteration codes only (never the tracings or images,
  non-commercial, the CEIPP corpus and kohaumotu.org credited) and
  files it honestly as its **first undeciphered script** — glyph
  sequences searchable as sequences, with no reading imposed.

The Pacific portions of the eBible scripture corpus join them on the
new shelf, so the desk opens with the islands' largest historical
press corpus, their most enigmatic script, and their living scripture
translations side by side.

The library stands at **2,921,684 documents / 134,417,291 passages
from 360 live sources in 291 language codes**.
