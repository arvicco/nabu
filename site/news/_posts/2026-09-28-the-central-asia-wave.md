---
title: "The Central Asia wave: two new desks on the Silk Road"
date: 2026-09-28 21:30:00 +0000
description: >-
  Six new sources open the library's Middle Iranian, Turkic and
  Mongolic lanes — Khotanese manuscripts from the British Library,
  the Invisible East documents, a trilingual Sasanian inscription,
  Chagatai manuscripts from Lund, and the Secret History of the
  Mongols — with two new research desks, a rhyme-dictionary
  crosswalk, and an adoption ledger for the whole registry.
---

The library turned east this week: the corridor between Iran and
China — Khotan, Sogdiana, Bactria, the Tarim oases, the Mongol
steppe — went from nearly empty to a working wing, and two new desks
opened to serve it: the **Turkologist** and the **Mongolist**, the
library's twenty-sixth and twenty-seventh research axes.

**The Iranian east.** Three sources anchor the Middle Iranian lane.
Skjærvø's *Khotanese Manuscripts from Chinese Turkestan in the
British Library* (Zenodo, CC BY 4.0) contributes 2,357 manuscript
records with 14,574 transliterated lines — the first bulk Khotanese
text anywhere in the library. The upstream file is a self-declared
draft with about a hundred unclosed tags; rather than let one broken
record poison its neighbors, the parser isolates each manuscript
record as its own fragment and recovers it independently, so damage
stays local and censused. Beside it, Oxford's **Invisible East
Digital Corpus** (Zenodo, CC BY 4.0) brings 1,316 catalog records of
documents from the medieval Islamicate East — New Persian, Sogdian,
Bactrian, Judeo-Persian, Middle Persian, Arabic — 481 of them with
folio transcriptions, plus typed dates and located toponyms that
feed the timeline and places lanes directly. And the **Kaʿba-ye
Zartošt inscription** (ŠKZ) arrives sentence-aligned in all three of
its versions — Middle Persian, Parthian, Greek — Šābuhr I's res
gestae as the library's first Parthian document, fifty lines each
readable against its two siblings.

**The Turki manuscripts.** The Turkologist's desk opens on **ATMO**
— the Annotated Turki Manuscripts from the Jarring Collection Online
(Indiana University and Lund University Library, CC BY-SA 4.0):
eighteen 18th–20th century Eastern Turki manuscripts transcribed
line by line, Perso-Arabic text with a full Latin transliteration on
every line, and even the marginalia and endorsements served with
their provenance marked. Beside it sit new Wiktionary extracts for
Old Turkic, Old Uyghur and Chagatai, and the first Turkic treebank —
Old Turkic clausal examples under Universal Dependencies.

**The Secret History.** The Mongolist's desk opens on John C.
Street's romanized text of the *Secret History of the Mongols* (v24,
2013), hosted by Monumenta Altaica — use granted by the site's
maintainer by email, with Street's own copyright notice carried
verbatim on every surface, in the spirit of the notice that the late
professor "always wanted free propagation of his works." One
document, 3,523 lines at Street's own line numbering, with section
markers and footnote pointers preserved as annotations. The kaikki
Manchu extract (2,577 entries) joins the same desk's
Mongolic-Tungusic lane, and Tocharian A and B dictionary extracts
land alongside for the Tarim's other language family.

**And the Slavic south.** One outlier rode the wave: **Scripta
Bulgarica** (Bulgarian Academy of Sciences, CC BY-NC-SA), 124
medieval Bulgarian monuments — the Bitola inscription, charters,
damaskini — each text carrying its printed-edition citation as
provenance. The fragile upstream was mirrored snapshot-first, so the
texts are safe whatever happens to the hosting.

**A rhyme dictionary learns its descendant.** The *Qieyun* (601 CE)
and the *Guangyun* (1008 CE) — the two poles of the Chinese
rime-dictionary tradition — are now cross-linked small rime by small
rime: 3,359 curated correspondences from the restoration project's
own concordance table, so a lookup in one dictionary shows its
counterpart in the other. The numbering shift the Guangyun's added
rimes introduce is exactly why the upstream table, not arithmetic,
is the authority — and the twenty-six rimes present only in a rival
restoration are counted honestly rather than guessed.

**The adoption ledger.** Housekeeping with teeth: the source
registry is now alphabetically ordered by construction, and every
source carries an explicit adoption date, backfilled from the
project's own history. `nabu list --by-adopted` reads the library's
growth as a timeline — newest arrivals first — and a standing census
now flags any source whose fetched metadata carries date-, place- or
genre-shaped fields that were never mined into the library's axes,
so unmined ore stays visible until it's either extracted or
explicitly declared out of scope.
