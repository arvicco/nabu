The library's Buddhist desk has read the dharma in Pali, Sanskrit,
and Chinese; as of today it reads it in Tibetan. **The complete Derge
canon** — the Kangyur's 1,200 texts and the Tengyur's 3,362, some
1.36 million woodblock lines — arrives in Esukhia's Public-Domain
exact-representation of the Degé blocks, cited by Tohoku number at
folio-line grain, mistakes of the carvers preserved as carved.

Around the canon, the full apparatus of a working desk, the
twenty-third: **84000's English translations** (388 texts at the
Reading Room's own citation grain), the **Old Tibetan documents** —
OTDO's 413 critical editions spanning the Dunhuang scrolls, the
imperial pillar inscriptions, and five texts in Old Zhangzhung —
the **Annals and Chronicle** annotated and paired line-by-line with
Dotson's translation, the SOAS gold corpus, and a lexicon shelf
anchored by the **Mahāvyutpatti**, the ninth-century imperial
glossary whose 9,379 entries bridge Sanskrit, Tibetan and Chinese —
so `define bodhisattva` and `define bodhisattvaḥ` now reach the same
entries through a stem-aware Sanskrit lookup.

The phase's charge was that Buddhist alignment "work to the fullest
extent," and the honest version turned out better than the easy one.
Document-grain translation edges join every Kangyur text to its
84000 translation; the cross-canon parallels graph (195,287 edges)
already pairs 261 Pali suttas with their Chinese āgama counterparts;
and where a first look said the citation schemes were irreconcilable,
a second look found 84000's TEI carries **33,073 inline Degé folio
anchors** — so `show <kangyur-text>:1b --parallel` renders the
Tibetan woodblock lines beside their English folio block, coverage
honestly labeled, in either direction. What was refused is as
deliberate as what was built: no fake line pairing, no
translation-equivalents dressed up as cognates.

Desk cards now name their languages with counts, language cards name
their desks, and the census stands at **968,182 documents / 68.3
million passages in 121 language codes**, 1.4 million dictionary
entries across 103 shelves.

Local, license-honest, rebuildable — from Sumer to the Land of Snows.
