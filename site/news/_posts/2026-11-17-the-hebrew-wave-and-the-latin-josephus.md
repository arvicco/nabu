  The rabbinic and documentary Hebrew shelves grow by four sources and

---

The weeks since the Greek canon landed belonged mostly to the Hebrew
shelves — a connected wave of sources, registry work and shelf
widening that changes what the library can say about the rabbinic and
documentary record.

**The Hebrew wave, by its parts:**

- **The vocalized Babylonian Talmud** (Dicta) — 37 tractate documents
  / 78,267 passages with full nikud, in both plene and defective
  spelling lanes. Search the Bavli with vowels.
- **The Dicta library** — 1,204 AI-OCR'd rabbinic printed books /
  193,335 passages: responsa, commentaries, halakha, derashot,
  Chassidut, the long tail of the printed rabbinic tradition, with
  the medieval layer staged as medieval Hebrew on the timeline.
- **The Princeton Geniza Project** — 9,848 documentary editions /
  303,860 passages from the Cairo Geniza: letters, lists, legal
  deeds and accounts of a thousand years of Mediterranean life,
  the majority in Judeo-Arabic.
- **The Samaritan Pentateuch** (CACCHT, Copenhagen) — the five books
  / 5,841 passages in the Samaritan tradition's own text, kept
  distinct from the Masoretic text it diverges from.

Two of those claims needed language codes that did not exist here
before, so the registry grew with the wave: **Judeo-Arabic** and
**Samaritan Hebrew** are now first-class anchors (351 in the
registry), each with its researched classification notes — the
Geniza's majority language and the Samaritan reading tradition no
longer ride approximate codes.

**The midrash shelf widened** on the same sitting: Midrash Tanchuma
(both recensions), Pesikta d'Rav Kahana, Pesikta Rabbati, Pirkei
d'Rabbi Eliezer, Yalkut Shimoni, Otzar Midrashim and Ein Yaakov
join the Rabbah collections and the halakhic midrashim already
held. And the Sefaria shelves now load their German and Hungarian
translations — thirteen translation documents that were previously
set aside for want of a language mapping.

**Josephus arrived in Latin.** The Latin Josephus project's critical
transcriptions — the Antiquities, the Jewish War, Contra Apionem and
the late-antique De excidio — are live as 35 documents / 3,526
passages of late Latin, the dress in which medieval Europe actually
read Josephus for a millennium. Beside him, the **Digital
Prosopography of the Roman Republic**: 4,876 persons with their
offices and dates, joining the person index that already reads the
classical corpora.

Elsewhere in the same stretch: **eight comparative dictionaries**
landed from the Lexibank CLDF collections; the **Greek–Latin
alignment layer** gained the SC–TLG reference edges; and the two
paced crawls kept their steady pace — the NDL kotenseki collection
of digitized Japanese classics now holds 71,838 documents, the
Wellcome medical-history volumes 964.

The library stands at **2,913,884 documents / 134,069,213 passages
from 353 live sources in 289 language codes**. Next on the bench:
the apparatus layer around the Greek New Testament — the tagged,
collated and witness-level editions that sit behind the text the
library already holds — surveyed in depth and queued for intake.
