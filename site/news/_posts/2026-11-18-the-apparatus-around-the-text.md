The last news item closed with the apparatus layer around the Greek
New Testament "surveyed in depth and queued for intake." It is no
longer queued.

**Three word-level Greek NT editions landed in one wave:**

- **STEPBible TAGNT** (Tyndale House, Cambridge) — 27 books / 7,958
  verses / 142,095 tagged words: the Translators Amalgamated Greek
  NT, the only free cross-edition apparatus. The text is the union
  of NA27/28, Tyndale House, SBL, Tregelles, Byzantine,
  Westcott–Hort and the Textus Receptus, and every word carries
  exactly which editions attest it. The TR-only verses are here with
  their attestation on them — search the doxology of the Lord's
  Prayer and the word rows tell you who prints it.
- **OpenGNT** — 27 books / 7,941 verses / 138,013 tagged words: an
  NA28-equivalent text whose every word carries lemma, Robinson
  morphology, extended Strong's and Louw–Nida numbers, with the
  NA28-versus-majority readings noted word by word.
- **MACULA Greek** (Clear Bible / Biblica) — 27 books / 7,939 verses
  / 137,741 tagged words over the same SBLGNT text the library
  already serves, as a deliberate annotation edition beside it:
  syntax roles, semantic frames, participant referents and a lexical
  English gloss on every word.

With the SBLGNT and PROIEL witnesses already held, the library now
reads the Greek New Testament in five editions — and for the first
time it can answer not just *what the text says* but *which editions
say it*, word by word.

**The piyyut and Andalusian canon arrived beside them.** Project
Ben-Yehuda is Hebrew's largest volunteer digitization; its
public-domain dump's pre-modern layer — upstream's own "antiquity
and piyyut" and "Middle Ages and Renaissance" bands, everything to
1700 — is now held: **6,043 works / 171,359 passages** by 206
authors, from Yannai and Qillir's liturgical poetry through the
Andalusian golden age (Shmuel HaNagid, Ibn Gabirol, Yehuda Halevi,
both Ibn Ezras) to Alharizi's maqamas and the Yemenite diwan of
Shabazi. The nikud is kept byte-verbatim — pointed Hebrew searches
land on pointed text — and every hit carries the volunteers'
requested credit. The medieval band rides the timeline as medieval
Hebrew, with upstream's period bands as composition-era envelopes.

The library stands at **2,920,008 documents / 134,264,410 passages
from 357 live sources in 289 language codes**.
