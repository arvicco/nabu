Two additions this week, one for the reader and one for the signs.

**The reference shelf grew by forty-two works.** The classic
apparatus of pre-Roman Italy is now on the shelf in its original
editions: Buck's *Grammar of Oscan and Umbrian*, von Planta's
two-volume *Grammatik der oskisch-umbrischen Dialekte*, Conway's
*Italic Dialects*, Pauli's *Altitalische Forschungen* (the
north-Etruscan alphabets, the Lemnos stele, the Veneti), Bréal's
*Tables Eugubines*, Zvetaieff's *Sylloge inscriptionum Oscarum*, and
Fabretti's *Corpus inscriptionum italicarum* — beside Bakkum's
open-access *Latin Dialect of the Ager Faliscus*, Cleasby-Vigfusson's
*Icelandic-English Dictionary*, the second volume of Thurneysen's
*Handbuch des Alt-Irischen* (completing the held first), and all
twenty-six articles of *Rasenna: Journal of the Center for Etruscan
Studies*. Public-domain scans, fetched from the Internet Archive and
the UMass repository with full provenance per item.

**Every Egyptian sign card now carries a third witness.** The AED
corpus ships a per-text hieroglyphic transcription for nearly
fourteen thousand texts — token by token, in plain Unicode
hieroglyphs. The library now censuses them into a precompiled
per-sign table (794 distinct signs, 725,856 occurrences, damaged
readings honestly included), so `nabu char 𓈖` answers instantly with
the sign's reach across *texts* beside its existing counts across
annotated *passages*: three corpora, three counting shapes, one card.
