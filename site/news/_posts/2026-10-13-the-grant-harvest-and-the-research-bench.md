A hundred thousand documents and a change of character. The last
three phases added shelves the way the library always has — but they
also added something new: instruments for *statistical* research
across everything the shelves hold.

**The whole Tocharian B corpus.** The West Tocharian manuscripts sit
behind a members-only gate at TITUS, and the library now walks
through it politely: a credentials seam stores the granted password
once (set interactively, never echoed, never in history) and every
fetch carries it. What came back was more than expected. The
"Tocharian B" chain turned out to run 4,349 pages — the Berlin
Turfan and SHT series, then the London India Office and Paris
Pelliot collections, one "Next part" link at a time — and after two
rounds of parser work the whole chain parses: **4,250 documents /
43,480 passages**, with the data-entry editors of all three
collections credited on every card. The honesty ledger is short and
documented: eighteen pages stay quarantined, most carrying text
with no line citations to mint. And two of them turned out not to
be Tocharian at all — Maue's handlist of Tumshuqese manuscripts
claims both leaves (one is the Tumshuqese *Araṇemijātaka*, and the
leaf itself spells the hero's name), so the library's first two
Tumshuq Saka documents join under their own code.

**TAMILEX, a day after the ask.** The Hamburg Academy project's
critical editions of the classical Tamil corpus — the Caṅkam
anthologies, the Tirukkuṟaḷ with its commentators, Tolkāppiyam, the
Nālāyirat Tivviyap Pirapantam — carry a per-word *wordsplit* layer
(segmentation, gloss, grammatical role, clitics) that exists
nowhere else for Old Tamil. The maintainers confirmed CC BY-NC-SA
for the whole estate, and the next morning the library held **7,830
documents / 14,972 passages** with **262,093 gold word-level
rows** mirroring the project's own indexer — and re-syncs tracking
upstream's practically daily edits.

**New scripts on the shelves.** The Balinese Wikisource's lontar
transcriptions arrived manuscript-direct — **1,252 palm-leaf
manuscripts** in Balinese script, no printed edition in between —
beside Gujarati bhakti prints, Jawi-script Malay manuscripts from
Leiden and the British Library, and the Thai Collected Chronicles.
Then the Gurmukhī canon: the Shabad OS database's **141,264
scripture lines** at line grain — the Gurū Granth Sāhib, the Vārāṅ
of Bhāī Gurdās — with the registry growing a Punjabi anchor, a
granthic Old Punjabi stage for the Sant Bhāṣā written language, and
Gurmukhi joining the script checkers. South India deepened too:
the DHARMA conversions of the printed *South Indian Inscriptions*
volumes (589 license-marked inscriptions with their original ASI
editors credited) and Project Madurai's **755 classical Tamil
e-texts at 1.2 million lines**.

**The research bench.** The reading instruments were always good;
the statistics instruments barely existed. Now: `nabu export`
writes research tables (documents with their full axes, the links
graph, the lemma attestation matrix) with a **license manifest**
beside every file — each export names every source it drew on and
its license class, and one flag cuts a provably redistributable
set. `nabu lemma` answers attestation profiles from precompiled
rollups — first and last century with the date's provenance class,
gold and silver curves shown separately — and `lang --curve` and
`lang --mixing` serve the demography and multilingualism questions
the same way, instantly. A dating lane joined for model output:
estimates live in a shelf behind an owner-controlled gate, project
as visibly-flagged *silver* dates that never mix into surfaces
promising upstream dates, and `search --date-basis` filters by
provenance class — the holdout discipline for dating research as a
flag. And the text-reuse machinery grew tuned per-language
profiles with resumable chunking; along the way the census found
that Chinese parallels had been silently empty (character n-grams
never matched the index's unbroken runs — fixed through the CJK
bigram index), and the library's **first full-scale Latin reuse
run** — the classical canon probed against the Patrologia — is
mining as this post goes up.

The ledger line: the library stands at **2.37 million documents,
112.5 million passages, 208 live sources** across 28 research
desks — a quarter-million documents and three writing systems
richer than it was ten days ago, with every grant, census and
quarantine recorded where a reader can check it.
