Two development windows closed today, and together they are the
largest single-day growth in the library's history: **2,609,689
documents / 121,507,867 passages across 269 live sources** (census 2
November 2026), up 1.7 million passages in a day.

**The classical Persian library, whole.** The operator of
[Ganjoor](https://ganjoor.net), the Persian poetry reference site,
granted reuse of its data the day of the ask, and within hours its
classical cone was on the shelves: **212 poets / 126,165 poems / 1,453,355
couplet-passages**, from Rudaki and Ferdowsi through Nezami, ʿAṭṭār,
Rumi, Saʿdi and Hafez to the Qajar divans, every poem dated by its
poet's life and most placed. Beside it landed the library's first New
Persian dictionary — English Wiktionary's Persian lexicon (20,350
entries whose etymologies reach back through Middle and Old Persian) —
so the new texts arrived with their lookup layer in the same window.

**Byzantine Greek, a shelf from nothing.** The Calfa/GREgORI
Patrologia Graeca brought 33 volumes of Migne read by machine — 6.8
million lemmatized Greek tokens of Photius, Psellos, Skylitzes,
Zonaras, Choniates, Gregoras and Palamas, uncorrected OCR declared as
such — where the library previously held no medieval Greek at all.

**The Semitic and biblical wave.** The ETCBC's Targum corpus (47
manuscripts of Aramaic targums with gold lemmas, the Cairo Genizah
fragments among them) and Syriac corpus (31 Peshitta books with
morphology), the Göttingen Hexapla database (Aquila, Symmachus and
Theodotion beside the Septuagint, 169,420 tokens), and the 1688
Bucharest Bible — the first complete Romanian Bible, in its original
Cyrillic — which also brought a Romanian branch to the lect registry.

**Inner Asia and the excavated past.** BDRC's page-aligned Tibetan
transcriptions (1,160 etext volumes / 99,571 woodblock and manuscript
pages, each page tied to its scan, fetched text-only) and the first
Warring States and Han bamboo-slip manuscripts: graph-level
transcriptions of the Guodian and Peking University Laozi, the Anhui
Odes and the Tsinghua chapters, every line aligned to its received
text.

**Dictionaries and instruments.** Cleasby–Vigfusson and Zoëga give
Old Norse its dictionary layer (65,158 entries); the 1914 Haga
biographical dictionary adds 54,558 Japanese entries; the Göttingen
RuneS database brings 613 runic finds read glyph by glyph; the
Paramāra inscriptions and Comenius's Czech devotional prose round out
the wave. Two long-standing lookup defects fell in the same window:
Welsh queries now reach the GPC under every spelling of the language's
code, and the OSHB lexicon's 1,562 Aramaic entries answer Aramaic
queries instead of hiding behind the shelf's Hebrew label.

**The morning window.** Before the wave, today's first gate delivered
the demo image's release rail (the public image is now multi-arch —
amd64 installs work — with a workflow that builds, smokes and
publishes it at release moments), a **silver scansion lane** (Latin
and Greek metrical analysis validated at 88.1% and 94.6% agreement
against held gold scansions, projected nowhere until deliberately
run), the Church Slavonic Elizabeth Bible (76 books / 36,000 verses,
verse-aligned to the library's Bible works), the Penn Parsed Corpus
of Historical Yiddish (63 hand-parsed texts, 1478–1959), and a
column-anchored bridge from the Celtic dictionary shelf into Holder's
*Alt-celtischer Sprachschatz* — whose volume I was fetched page by
page from the Bavarian State Library's open IIIF service the same
day.

The lect registry grew to meet the material: one aggregated package
of 28 anchors is under review — a twelve-leaf Pacific spine, Vietnamese
with its Nôm-era stages, Classical Mongolian and Written Oirat,
Tangut, Yiddish with its Weinreich stages, and Romanian — each with
researched classification notes. As always, the numbers above are the
generated census of the live catalog, not hand-kept claims.
